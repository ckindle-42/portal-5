The code seeder builds WHAT units from the repo's Python modules, grouping
them by top-level subsystem and citing the first files of each group with the
current commit. It is the machine-derived tier that the coverage gate excludes
from counting.

## Why

The WHAT units are the mechanical "what exists" layer of the spine: for every
subsystem, a unit naming its files. Because they are a projection of the repo,
they are excluded from the coverage numerator — counting them would grade the
generator against its own output, the circularity the coverage gate exists to
avoid. They are regenerated by the maintenance loop whenever the repo moves.

## Interfaces

`seed_code(dry_run)` returns the WHAT units grouped by subsystem;
`discover_python_files` is the shared eligibility definition the coverage
gate mirrors.

## Gotchas

Only subsystems with two or more files get a unit, and citations are capped
at the first five files — a large subsystem's unit is a pointer, not an
inventory.
