## 1. Splunk Enterprise Security RBA

Splunk's ES 7.3 documentation [updated 2025-07-14](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-7/risk-based-alerting/7.3/introduction/how-risk-based-alerting-works-in-splunk-enterprise-security) describes risk rules producing risk events in the risk index, associated with a risk object (system, user, or other); risk incident rules aggregate activity on an object and generate risk notables. A score over a window is the sum of attributed risk event scores. Risk factors can add to or multiply the score using context such as asset or identity; these are configuration choices, not observed probabilities. The [risk score explanation, ES 7.3, 2025-07-14](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-7/risk-based-alerting/7.3/introduction/how-risk-scores-work-in-splunk-enterprise-security) distinguishes a modifier from the resulting notable. The Risk data model is a search/acceleration representation, not a guarantee that all upstream data or time ranges are complete; see [risk data model, ES 7.3](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-7/risk-based-alerting/7.3/configure-risk-based-alerting/configure-the-risk-data-model) (URL attempted; exact details unverified if unavailable).

Incident conditions are not universally score sums: the ES documentation gives separate examples of score over a daily window, distinct sourcetypes over a multi-day window, and multiple ATT&CK tactics over another window. Those examples use configured thresholds, mappings, and windows. A threshold-free analogue can sort all entity-window groups by a declared total order of distinct evidence families and within-family surprise, preserving the component evidence; taking a finite investigation budget is still a resource cutoff. It is not a threshold-free equivalent to generating a discrete alert, nor evidence of maliciousness.

**Noise and inflation:** repeated detections or overlapping rules can add the same underlying activity repeatedly; distinct rule names or source labels alone do not prove independent evidence. This is a design inference from the sum semantics, not a quantified Splunk failure rate. ES documents repeated notables and [dynamic throttling, ES 7.3, 2025-07-14](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-7/risk-based-alerting/7.3/best-practices/suppressing-false-positives-using-alert-throttling): compare current and prior results using object, detection and threat context, with explicit warning that throttling can hide genuine threats. Preserve original observations and provenance when deduplicating; suppression should not erase history. A primary RBA-in-a-box or .conf source establishing a general double-counting rate or a validated correction was not verified; no rate is claimed. Elastic's discounting of repeated alerts below is a concrete vendor alternative, not proof of an optimal fix.

## 2. Comparable designs (vendor descriptions unless stated)

- [Elastic entity risk scoring, current documentation](https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/security/advanced-entity-analytics/entity-risk-scoring): hosts, users and services accumulate open/acknowledged alert risk, including building blocks, with recency and repeated-alert handling; risk is normalized and categorised. Building blocks can feed downstream detection without directly cluttering normal alert views. Formula, windows and rule-assigned risk are vendor policy, not threshold-free inference. Confirm installed version before transferring exact defaults. [Building-block rules, current documentation](https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/security/detect-and-alert/building-block-rules).
- [Microsoft Sentinel UEBA, updated 2026-05-14](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/sentinel/identify-threats-with-entity-behavior-analytics): user, host, IP and application profiles compare behavior to entity history, peers and organization; behavioral records and anomaly scores are distinct. [UEBA reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/sentinel/ueba-reference) describes event-level investigation priority. [Detection types](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/sentinel/threat-detection) describe anomaly-rule parameters and Fusion's opaque correlation of lower-fidelity alerts and events; availability varies by integration. [Fusion](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/sentinel/fusion) uses mapped entities and tactics for eligible scheduled alerts. Do not infer its unpublished ranking formula.
- [Google SecOps composite detections, current documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/chronicle/docs/detection/composite-detections): rules join earlier detections with optional events, metrics and entity-graph context over a match window, e.g. same user or host. Rules define conditions and may emit risk scores; this is correlation, not a documented universal ranker. Vendor claims of reduced false positives are not independent measured results.
- [Sigma correlation specification, 2025](https://sigmahq.io/sigma-specification/specification/sigma-correlation-rules-specification.html): referenced rules, group-by fields and timespan support event counts, distinct values, temporal co-occurrence and ordered sequences. Conditions and windows are authored; Sigma specifies matching rather than a universal ranking algorithm. Backend support varies ([current guide](https://sigmahq.io/docs/meta/correlations)).
- [Exabeam behavioral analytics feature brief, 2022](https://www.exabeam.com/wp-content/uploads/FEATUREBRIEF-Behavioral-Analytics.pdf): user/entity timelines collect events and alerts, assign risk to anomalies, and aggregate to prioritize timelines. Its [Advanced Analytics guide, 2024](https://docs.exabeam.com/private/paligo-output/en/cloud-delivered-advanced-analytics/all/user-guide/153636-Exabeam__User_Guide-pdf-en.pdf) also describes asset sessions and entity risk across sessions. Exact model/calibration and generalization are not established by these vendor descriptions.

## 3. Hunting methods and evidence

[Splunk SURGe PEAK, 2023](https://img.etb2bimg.com/files/cp/2220/cdoc-1701859248-The%20PEAK%20Threat%20Hunting%20Framework.pdf) describes baseline hunts, stack counting, time-series analysis, investigation and feedback. A stack orders distinct artifacts by frequency; least-frequency is a cue, not an incident. First-seen is relative to available historical coverage, not the first occurrence ever. Peer comparison asks whether the entity differs from comparable peers; burst detection asks whether the time series deviates from its own history. Each needs a defensible population and time baseline; time-varying roles, missing data, new hosts and benign updates generate outliers. The [SANS threat-hunting survey, 2019](https://www.sans.org/media/analyst-program/2019-threat-hunting-survey-differing-experienced-hunters-39220.pdf) gives a practitioner example of stacking executables and stresses experienced analyst interpretation; it does not establish detection sensitivity or false-positive cost for a particular production population.

TaHiTI is a threat-intelligence-led hunt methodology, rather than proof that a purely blind ranker works; [TaHiTI authors' whitepaper, 2018, archived copy](https://github.com/threat-hunting/awesome_Threat-Hunting/blob/master/Threat%20Intelligence/Files/TaHiTI-Threat-Hunting-Methodology-whitepaper-THLink.pdf). PEAK includes baseline hunts as well as hypothesis-driven hunts; no method is assumption-free. Verified primary sources here do not provide comparable real-intrusion yield and analyst-hours/false-positive cost for each of stacking, first-seen, peers and bursts. Those quantities remain unknown, including in this environment; do not turn illustrative use cases into measured claims.

## 4. Log structure and anomaly research

`punct` retains punctuation, discarding most lexical and semantic information: different messages can collide and variable punctuation can split the same event type. It is a cheap indexed proxy, not Drain or a parsed template. [Drain, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICWS.2017.13) uses an online fixed-depth parse tree; [Spell, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDM.2016.0096) uses longest common subsequence; [LogCluster, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/CNSM.2015.7367381) clusters patterns. These entail parser choices and processing costs, even without trained ML. [DeepLog, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3133956.3134015) models sequences with a trained LSTM; [LogAnomaly, 2019](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0658.pdf) combines sequential and quantitative information. They address system log anomalies, not automatically malicious activity.

Independent work on [ten real online-service datasets, 2021](https://netman.aiops.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/logstudy.pdf) evaluates several unsupervised methods and identifies practical challenges; performance and operational assumptions must be read per dataset, not generalized to security intrusions. [Le and Zhang, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04301) revisit deep-learning evaluations and show sensitivity to selection, grouping, imbalance, noise and early detection. [Large-scale parsing evaluation, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10828) motivates Loghub-2.0 and evaluation beyond small curated subsets. These studies support measuring parsing and end-to-end utility; they do not prove that a particular parser or ML approach is cheap or effective on this deployment. Indexed counts and frequency ranks require no model training; the actual query and transport cost remains to be measured.

## 5. Splunk mechanics and limits

Documentation version matters; check the actual deployment version, segmentation and `limits.conf`. [Enterprise 10.4 `tstats`, updated 2026-05-14](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise/spl-search-reference/10.4/search-commands/tstats) uses indexed fields/TSIDX or accelerated data models. `BY` omits events with missing grouping fields unless `fillnull_value` is specified. Aggregates over indexed rows can be exact for the indexed population while excluding missing fields, unindexed content, incomplete buckets, permissions or censored output. `PREFIX(key=)` operates on raw indexed segments, not arbitrary extracted fields: the prefix must match an indexed segment, and the delimiter is a minor breaker such as `=` or `:`; major breakers in prefix or value are unsupported. Segmentation, quoting, whitespace, case and compound values matter; a sampled key is not a census. [TERM semantics, Enterprise 10.4](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise/search/search-manual/10.4/search-primer/use-case-and-term-to-match-phrases) depend on indexed segmentation; it can match a minor-breaker-containing term bounded by major breakers, not arbitrary substrings. Confirm the exact version and term forms on deployment.

[Default fields, Cloud 9.3.2408](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-cloud-platform/manage-knowledge-objects/knowledge-management-manual/9.3.2408/fields-and-field-extractions/use-default-fields) describes `punct` as a punctuation pattern; [props.conf Enterprise 9.4](https://splunk.portal.heretto.com/en/splunk-enterprise/administer/admin-manual/9.4/configuration-file-reference/9.4.0-configuration-file-reference/props.conf) documents `ANNOTATE_PUNCT` and its default. The exact truncation length and character/byte convention were **not verified from an authoritative versioned source**. Stability across ingestion configuration, versions, line breaks and truncation is not established; do not assume a fixed length or one-to-one template identity. Literal equality must avoid search wildcard interpretation and be checked against raw results.

[Enterprise 10.6 `walklex`, updated 2026-09-09](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise/spl-search-reference/10.6/search-commands/walklex) enumerates per-bucket lexicon terms, can be inconsistent and excludes hot buckets; it is not a complete or conservative exact counter. `tstats prestats=true` produces intermediate state for a consuming `stats`, `chart` or `timechart`, not an ordinary final row format ([10.4 `tstats`](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise/spl-search-reference/10.4/search-commands/tstats)). [Data-model acceleration, Enterprise 10.0](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise/manage-knowledge-objects/knowledge-management-manual/10.0/use-data-summaries-to-accelerate-searches/accelerate-data-models) builds TSIDX summaries asynchronously; `summariesonly=true` can exclude unsummarized history. [Summary indexing and `collect`, Enterprise 10.4](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise/search/spl-search-reference/10.4/search-commands/collect) writes results to an index; scheduled gaps, duplicate collection and retention need reconciliation. It is a write and is outside this worker's scope.

[Enterprise 10.4 limits.conf](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise/administer/admin-manual/10.4/configuration-file-reference/10.4.0-configuration-file-reference/limits.conf) documents `[tstats] chunk_size` and memory behavior; `[stats] max_mem_usage_mb`, `maxresultrows` and `max_keymap_rows` govern intermediate processing/spill. `[searchresults] maxresultrows` and API pagination/output limits are distinct; do not treat an in-memory spill setting as a final output cap. Scheduler and role search concurrency are configured separately; [workload and search concurrency, Enterprise 10.4](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise/administer/manage-users-and-security/10.4/manage-users-and-roles/authorize-user-roles-with-capabilities) (exact settings and effective deployment values unverified). Capture job completion, warnings, result counts and every page; neither a successful HTTP status nor a timing proves completeness.

## 6. Combining and multiple testing

Raw novelty, counts and burst scores have different scales. Rank aggregation avoids asserting they are commensurate; the [robust rank aggregation paper, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btr709) compares ranks against a random-list null and handles incomplete lists under its assumptions. An ordinal consensus can be used without claiming statistical significance, but missing observations, ties, population sizes and correlated rankers require explicit treatment. Diversity counts are interpretable but a common underlying event can populate several kinds; count provenance families, not presumed independent labels. Conversely, lexicographic diversity-first ranking can bury a strong single signal.

Fisher's `-2 sum(log p)` has a chi-square null for independent valid p-values; [empirical Brown method, 2016](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/32/17/i430/2450768) addresses dependence by estimating covariance. Stouffer combines normal-transformed valid p-values, optionally weighted; correlation must enter its variance. Neither the existing heuristic z nor first-seen alone supplies a calibrated p-value. Calibration, nonstationarity, sparse history, dependence and selective sampling must be established before either method has inferential meaning. [Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x) controls expected false discovery proportion under stated assumptions given valid p-values and a chosen FDR level; dependence requires an appropriate justified procedure. FDR is a thresholded discovery decision, not a way to avoid a threshold. Ranking adjusted p-values or empirical ranks is possible, but neither guarantees that the top budget contains an intrusion.

## Mapping onto the P3 plan

| Layer | Evidence supports / contradicts / adds | Alternatives and five-property trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Observations | Indexed shared aggregates and per-entity histories fit baseline hunting; `punct` and sampled `PREFIX` are lossy. A whole-index query is not necessarily complete: null groups, hot/incomplete data, time bounds and retrieval limits matter. First-seen at the start of retention is left-censored; active-only baselines exclude meaningful idle periods. | Shape counts are repeatable if complete and ties fixed, and potentially quick/efficient; accuracy is only for the indexed grouping, correctness requires literal checks. Drain/Spell give richer patterns but add cost and parser errors. Peer/stack ranks can find cross-host rarity but require comparable peers and entity resolution; no guaranteed accuracy or speed. Raw-event scan improves semantic coverage but may exceed budgets. |
| Accumulation | Entity/time correlation and retained low-level findings are supported by vendor designs; unique kinds/sources are not independent. A shared value does not establish same actor, and common values can merge unrelated hosts. Fixed hour boundaries split episodes. | Diversity-first plus surprise is repeatable with total ordering and cheap after collection, but can overcount and down-rank single strong signals. Rank consensus is scale-free but ranking changes with population and missingness. Provenance-aware collapse and sliding/session windows may improve correctness but require more state and can cost time. P-values/FDR require calibration not shown here. |
| Notables/investigation | Budgeted drill-down with retained lineage is consistent with analyst workflows; a top-ranked group is a lead, not a confirmed notable. A shape-hour can still have too many events, collisions or omitted values; incomplete fetch must propagate. | Drill selected groups or explore a diverse set across rankings; both trade recall against a finite budget and selection policy. Exact raw-event checks improve correctness but not guaranteed quickness. The existing main-branch investigator is not proof that P2F's unmerged implementation is present or suitable. |

The plan's claims of exact full history and complete small censuses are conditional hypotheses, not established by the quoted timings. The five properties must be assessed separately: deterministic order does not imply accurate score, full coverage, semantic correctness, or speed. No choice above establishes real intrusion recall or false-positive cost.

## Read-only live probes for the orchestrator

Run only against an authorized index and a fixed absolute window in the Splunk search UI/API. Replace `<INDEX>`, `<EARLIEST>`, `<LATEST>`, `<KEY>`, `<HOST>`, `<PUNCT>` with actual, safely escaped values chosen from the data; preserve the completed job's messages, pagination counts and effective time bounds. These are SPL search bodies, not shell commands. Do not run `collect` or alter settings. The probes compare coverage, not intrusion outcomes.

1. **Null grouping loss.** Run each with identical absolute bounds:
   ```spl
   | tstats count where index=<INDEX> earliest=<EARLIEST> latest=<LATEST>
   ```
   ```spl
   | tstats fillnull_value="__MISSING__" count where index=<INDEX> earliest=<EARLIEST> latest=<LATEST> by host source sourcetype punct | stats sum(count) as grouped_count
   ```
   ```spl
   | tstats count where index=<INDEX> earliest=<EARLIEST> latest=<LATEST> by host source sourcetype punct | stats sum(count) as grouped_count
   ```
   Disagreement between total and either grouped sum requires a coverage explanation; a missing-field difference invalidates a blanket complete-history claim.
2. **Row retrieval and cardinality.** Compare the completed count of all paginated rows for:
   ```spl
   | tstats fillnull_value="__MISSING__" count where index=<INDEX> earliest=<EARLIEST> latest=<LATEST> by host punct _time span=1h
   ```
   with the server-side count and sum:
   ```spl
   | tstats fillnull_value="__MISSING__" count where index=<INDEX> earliest=<EARLIEST> latest=<LATEST> by host punct _time span=1h | stats count as rows sum(count) as events
   ```
   Mismatched row count/sum, warnings, partial job or failed pagination changes the plan's completeness assumption.
3. **PREFIX versus raw extraction for one observed key.** Choose a safe literal key with observed examples, not a fixed proposed method:
   ```spl
   | tstats count where index=<INDEX> earliest=<EARLIEST> latest=<LATEST> by PREFIX(<KEY>=) | stats sum(count) as indexed_matches
   ```
   ```spl
   search index=<INDEX> earliest=<EARLIEST> latest=<LATEST> | regex _raw="(^|[[:space:]])<KEY>=" | stats count as raw_key_events
   ```
   Compare the definitions and inspect cases with quotes, spaces and punctuation; unequal totals or ambiguous multi-occurrence semantics require a narrower coverage claim. Substitute an appropriately regex-escaped literal; the expressions are deliberately not presumed equivalent.
4. **Punctuation collision and literal drill.** Select one actual punctuation value; SPL-escape the string and use exact `where` equality:
   ```spl
   search index=<INDEX> host="<HOST>" earliest=<EARLIEST> latest=<LATEST> | where punct="<PUNCT>" | stats count as events dc(_raw) as distinct_raw by source sourcetype punct
   ```
   ```spl
   | tstats count where index=<INDEX> host="<HOST>" earliest=<EARLIEST> latest=<LATEST> by source sourcetype punct
   ```
   Compare the exact selected row and inspect actual events under the same bounds. Any mismatch, diverse unrelated messages, or unmanageable row count changes the proposed drill strategy. Exact distinct raw count does not by itself measure semantic collision.
5. **History edge and bucket boundaries.**
   ```spl
   | tstats count where index=<INDEX> earliest=<EARLIEST> latest=<LATEST> by host _time span=1h | stats min(_time) as first max(_time) as last count as active_hours sum(count) as events by host
   ```
   Hosts first appearing at the window edge or with sparse history require a censored/insufficient-baseline state; boundary-spanning activity requires comparing neighboring hours before treating windows as separate incidents. This query alone cannot distinguish retention truncation from a genuinely new host.

## Recommendation: hypotheses for review

Retain explainable observations with provenance and explicit coverage; test shape, value and peer signals as complementary, lossy views. Compare diversity-first against rank consensus and a single-signal-preserving ordering, with correlation-aware deduplication and deterministic ties. Validate aggregate completeness, literal drill coverage and actual analyst usefulness before committing to any ranker. Keep statistical significance and automated incident claims out until null calibration, dependence and operational false-positive cost are measured. These are hypotheses for the orchestrator's plan revision, not verdicts.
